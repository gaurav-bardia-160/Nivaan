The Official Framework: Guidelines for Indian Government Websites and Applications
What Are the GIGW Guidelines?
The primary official framework governing Government Website Development Guidelines in India is the Guidelines for Indian Government Websites and Applications, commonly referred to as the GIGW guidelines. These guidelines are issued and periodically updated by the National Informatics Centre under the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology, which is the primary government technology agency responsible for setting digital standards for government services in India.
The GIGW guidelines were first issued in 2009 and have been updated multiple times to reflect evolving technology standards, security requirements, accessibility obligations, and the growing importance of mobile-first design. The current version of the GIGW guidelines covers a comprehensive range of requirements that all government websites and web applications must meet, regardless of the government body they serve or the technology platform on which they are built.
The GIGW guidelines are mandatory for all central government ministry websites, department websites, public sector undertaking websites, and autonomous body websites. Many state governments have adopted the GIGW guidelines either directly or with state-specific modifications. Understanding the full scope of these guidelines is the starting point for any agency working on government digital projects.
The Digital India Initiative and Web Standards
The Digital India programme launched by the Government of India has significantly elevated the standards expected of government digital properties. Under Digital India, government websites are expected to be accessible to all citizens regardless of disability, available in Hindi and regional languages alongside English, optimized for mobile devices, secure against cyber threats, and designed to deliver genuinely useful government services rather than just providing information.
The Digital India framework reinforces and expands upon the GIGW guidelines, particularly in areas of mobile accessibility, multilingual content delivery, integration with national digital infrastructure including Aadhaar, DigiLocker, and the India Stack, and interoperability with other government systems and databases.

Domain and Hosting Requirements Under Government Website Development Guidelines in India
The Government Domain Naming Convention
One of the most foundational requirements in Government Website Development Guidelines in India is the domain naming convention for official government websites. All central government ministry and department websites must use the gov.in domain extension, which is managed by the National Internet Exchange of India under the authority of the Department of Telecommunications. State government websites use the nic.in domain or state-specific sub-domains under the gov.in structure.
The domain allocation process for government websites is managed through the National Informatics Centre, and agencies developing government websites must coordinate with the relevant government body and NIC to ensure that the correct domain is acquired and configured before the website goes live. Using any domain other than the prescribed government domain structure for an official government website is a violation of Government Website Development Guidelines in India that can result in the website not being recognized as an official government property.
Mandatory Government Cloud Hosting
Government Website Development Guidelines in India include specific and mandatory requirements about where government websites must be hosted. Central government websites must be hosted on MeghRaj, which is the Government Cloud infrastructure established by the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology. MeghRaj provides cloud computing services including infrastructure as a service, platform as a service, and software as a service specifically designed for government digital properties with the security, reliability, and data sovereignty requirements that government hosting demands.
The mandatory government cloud hosting requirement means that development agencies cannot host government websites on public commercial cloud platforms like AWS, Azure, or Google Cloud, or on shared hosting platforms, without specific authorization from the relevant government authority. MeghRaj hosting ensures that government data remains within India’s geographic boundaries in compliance with data localization requirements, is subject to government-grade security controls, and is backed by the National Informatics Centre’s technical infrastructure and support.
SSL Certificates and HTTPS Requirements
All government websites in India must use HTTPS rather than HTTP, secured with a valid SSL certificate. The GIGW guidelines mandate that all government websites implement SSL certificates from authorized certificate authorities, and that the entire website including all sub-pages and resources is served over HTTPS without any mixed content issues. This requirement reflects the fundamental security principle that all data transmitted between citizens and government services must be encrypted to protect personal information and transaction data from interception.

Accessibility Requirements: The Most Critical Technical Mandate
Web Content Accessibility Guidelines Compliance
The single most technically demanding and legally significant requirement in Government Website Development Guidelines in India is the mandatory compliance with the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines, known as WCAG, developed by the World Wide Web Consortium. Indian Government Website Development Guidelines require all government websites to meet at minimum WCAG 2.1 Level AA compliance, which is the internationally recognized standard for web accessibility.
WCAG compliance is not merely a technical checkbox. It is a legal and ethical obligation that ensures government digital services are accessible to all citizens, including those with visual impairments, hearing impairments, motor disabilities, cognitive disabilities, and other conditions that affect how individuals interact with digital content. In a country of India’s scale and diversity, accessibility compliance is directly connected to the constitutional principle of equal access to government services for all citizens.
The Four WCAG Principles for Government Websites
WCAG 2.1 is organized around four foundational principles that every government website must satisfy.
Perceivable
The Perceivable principle requires that all information and user interface components on the website must be presentable to users in ways they can perceive. This means all images must have alternative text descriptions that screen readers can convey to visually impaired users. All audio content must have text transcripts. All video content must have captions. Color alone must not be used to convey information because color-blind users may not perceive color-based distinctions. Text must have sufficient contrast against its background to be readable by users with low vision.
Operable
The Operable principle requires that all user interface components and navigation must be operable by users regardless of their input method. All functionality must be accessible using a keyboard alone, without requiring a mouse, because users with motor disabilities or those using assistive technology may rely exclusively on keyboard navigation. There must be sufficient time for users to read and complete tasks. Content must not include elements that flash rapidly in ways that could trigger seizures. Users must be able to navigate, find content, and determine where they are on the website.
Understandable
The Understandable principle requires that information and the operation of the website must be understandable to users. Text must be readable and understandable, with the language of the page identified in the HTML code so that screen readers and translation tools can process it correctly. The website must operate in predictable ways so users can understand how to interact with it. Input assistance must be provided to help users avoid and correct mistakes in forms and interactive elements.
Robust
The Robust principle requires that content must be robust enough to be interpreted reliably by a wide variety of user agents including assistive technologies. This means the HTML and CSS code of the website must be valid and standards-compliant, and the website must work correctly with current and future user agents including screen readers, refreshable Braille displays, and voice control software.
Accessibility Testing and Audit Requirements
Government Website Development Guidelines in India require that government websites undergo formal accessibility auditing and testing before launch and periodically after launch to verify ongoing WCAG 2.1 Level AA compliance. Accessibility testing involves both automated testing using specialized tools and manual testing with actual assistive technology users. Automated testing tools can identify many common accessibility issues but cannot catch all WCAG violations, making manual testing by accessibility specialists an essential component of the compliance process.
Development agencies delivering government websites must provide evidence of accessibility compliance in the form of accessibility audit reports and, where required, accessibility conformance statements that document the level of compliance achieved and any known exceptions.

Multilingual Requirements Under Government Website Development Guidelines in India
Hindi and Regional Language Mandates
Government Website Development Guidelines in India include specific requirements for multilingual content delivery. All central government websites must provide content in Hindi as a mandatory requirement, reflecting the constitutional status of Hindi as the official language of the Union. In addition to Hindi, government websites serving citizen populations with significant speakers of specific regional languages should provide content in those languages as well.
The multilingual requirement has significant technical implications for website development including the need for Unicode-compliant fonts and character encoding that correctly renders the full range of Indian script characters, right-to-left text support where applicable for languages like Urdu, font rendering optimization for Indian scripts on different devices and operating systems, and content management systems that support multilingual content workflows allowing content editors to maintain translations efficiently.
The Anuvadini and Government Translation Resources
The Government of India provides official translation resources and tools including the Anuvadini automated translation system developed by the Centre for Development of Advanced Computing for translating government content between English and Indian languages. Government website development guidelines encourage the use of these official translation resources for generating multilingual content while also specifying that automated translations must be reviewed and verified for accuracy before publication.
Development agencies working on government websites must implement content management systems that support multilingual content management and must ensure that all technical infrastructure including fonts, character encoding, and rendering is correctly configured for all languages in which the website will publish content.
Security Requirements in Government Website Development Guidelines in India
The Cyber Security Framework for Government Websites
Government Website Development Guidelines in India include comprehensive and technically demanding security requirements that go significantly beyond what is expected of commercial website development. Government websites are high-value targets for cybercriminals, hackers, and nation-state actors seeking to steal citizen data, disrupt government services, or compromise government systems. The security framework for government websites reflects this elevated threat environment.
The Indian Computer Emergency Response Team, known as CERT-In, under the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology, issues security guidelines for government websites and conducts security audits and penetration testing of government digital properties. CERT-In guidelines are incorporated into the GIGW framework and represent mandatory security standards for all government websites.
Mandatory Security Requirements for Government Websites
Secure Coding Practices
Government websites must be developed following secure coding practices that prevent the most common categories of web application vulnerabilities including SQL injection, cross-site scripting, cross-site request forgery, insecure direct object references, security misconfigurations, sensitive data exposure, and broken authentication. Development teams working on government websites must be trained in secure coding practices and must follow a secure development lifecycle that includes security requirements, threat modeling, security code review, and security testing at every stage of development.
Web Application Firewall
All government websites and web applications must be protected by a Web Application Firewall that monitors and filters HTTP traffic between the website and the internet, blocking malicious requests and attacks in real time. The WAF must be configured to the specific security requirements of the government website and must be maintained with current rule sets to protect against evolving attack techniques.
Regular Security Audits and Penetration Testing
Government Website Development Guidelines in India require that all government websites undergo regular security audits and penetration testing by authorized security testing agencies. The frequency of security audits depends on the criticality and sensitivity of the website, but at minimum, a comprehensive security audit must be conducted before initial launch and annually thereafter. Critical government websites and those handling sensitive citizen data must be audited more frequently.
Data Protection and Privacy Requirements
Government websites that collect personal data from citizens must implement comprehensive data protection measures consistent with India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. This includes obtaining valid consent for data collection, implementing data minimization principles, ensuring data security through encryption and access controls, providing citizens with rights over their personal data, and maintaining records of data processing activities.
The privacy policy published on every government website must accurately describe the data collected, the purpose of collection, the retention period, and the rights available to citizens regarding their personal data.

Content Standards and Quality Requirements
Accuracy and Currency of Government Content
Government Website Development Guidelines in India include specific requirements about the quality and currency of content published on government websites. Government websites have a special responsibility to citizens because citizens rely on them for accurate information about government services, entitlements, procedures, and legal requirements. Inaccurate, outdated, or misleading content on a government website can cause citizens to make incorrect decisions, miss deadlines, or fail to access services they are entitled to.
The GIGW guidelines specify that all government websites must have a clearly defined content governance structure including designated content owners responsible for the accuracy and currency of content in specific sections, a documented content review process with defined review frequencies, a mechanism for citizens to report inaccurate or outdated content, and a defined escalation process for addressing content quality issues.
Plain Language and Citizen-Centric Writing
Government Website Development Guidelines in India emphasize the importance of plain language writing on government websites. Government content historically has been characterized by complex bureaucratic language, legal jargon, and passive voice constructions that make it difficult for the average citizen to understand. The GIGW guidelines and the Digital India programme both emphasize that government website content must be written in clear, simple language that is accessible to citizens with a basic level of literacy in the relevant language.
Plain language requirements include using active voice rather than passive voice, avoiding jargon and technical terms without explanation, using short sentences and paragraphs, organizing content with clear headings and subheadings, and testing content comprehension with representative users.
File Format Standards for Government Content
Government Website Development Guidelines in India specify approved file formats for content published on government websites. Documents made available for download from government websites should be in open, widely accessible formats. The GIGW guidelines recommend the use of open document formats including PDF, ODF, and standard image formats for published documents, and specifically discourage the use of proprietary formats that require citizens to install specific commercial software to access government information.
Performance and Technical Standards
Page Load Speed Requirements
Government Website Development Guidelines in India include performance requirements specifying minimum page load speed standards for government websites. The GIGW guidelines set specific maximum page load times that government websites must meet, recognizing that many Indian citizens access government websites on low-bandwidth mobile connections, and slow-loading websites effectively exclude these citizens from accessing government services.
Meeting government website performance requirements demands careful attention to image optimization, code minification, server response time optimization, content delivery network implementation, and caching strategy. Development agencies must test government website performance across a range of network conditions including low-bandwidth mobile connections to ensure compliance with performance requirements.
Mobile Responsiveness Requirements
All government websites developed under current Government Website Development Guidelines in India must be fully mobile-responsive, providing an equivalent quality of experience on smartphone screens as on desktop screens. This requirement reflects the reality of India’s internet usage patterns, where mobile devices account for the overwhelming majority of internet access, particularly among citizens in semi-urban and rural areas who may have no access to desktop computers.
Mobile responsiveness goes beyond simply making the website display on a small screen. It requires thoughtful adaptation of navigation, form design, content layout, and interactive elements to ensure that all website functionality is accessible and usable on touch screen devices with varying screen sizes and input methods.
Browser Compatibility Requirements
Government Website Development Guidelines in India specify that government websites must function correctly across all major web browsers including Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Safari, and their mobile equivalents, as well as legacy browser versions that may still be in use among the citizen population. Browser compatibility testing must be conducted before launch and after any significant updates to verify that the website functions correctly across all required browser environments.
Intellectual Property and Legal Compliance for Government Website Development
IP Ownership in Government Website Development Contracts
One of the most important legal dimensions of Government Website Development Guidelines in India relates to intellectual property ownership. When a private company or agency develops a website for a government client, the question of who owns the intellectual property in the resulting website, including the code, the design, the content, and other original creative elements, must be clearly addressed in the development contract.
In most government development contracts, the standard position is that all intellectual property created in the course of the contract belongs to the government client rather than the developing agency. This means that the developing agency cannot reuse the same code, design patterns, or other elements developed for the government project in other commercial projects without explicit permission from the government client.
Development agencies working on government projects must ensure that their internal IP policies, their subcontractor agreements, and their employment contracts with developers are all aligned with the IP ownership terms of their government contracts. Failure to properly manage IP ownership in government development projects can create legal liability for the agency.

Licensing of Third-Party Components in Government Websites
Government websites frequently incorporate third-party software components including open source libraries, JavaScript frameworks, content management system plugins, and commercial software licenses. Government Website Development Guidelines in India require that all third-party components used in government websites are properly licensed for government use and that open source components are used in compliance with their license terms.
Some open source licenses, particularly copyleft licenses like the GPL, impose conditions on the distribution of software that incorporates GPL-licensed components. Development agencies must carefully assess the license terms of all open source components used in government website development to ensure that incorporation of these components does not create license compliance issues for the government client.

The GIGW Compliance Certification Process
Pre-Launch Compliance Verification
Before a government website developed by an external agency goes live, it must undergo a compliance verification process to confirm that it meets all applicable Government Website Development Guidelines in India. This verification process is typically conducted by the National Informatics Centre or by an authorized compliance assessment agency, and covers accessibility compliance, security compliance, performance compliance, content standards compliance, and technical standards compliance.
The compliance verification process involves submission of the website for technical assessment, provision of documentation including accessibility audit reports, security audit reports, and performance testing reports, and in some cases a formal presentation to the reviewing authority explaining how compliance requirements have been met.
Agencies that deliver government websites without completing the required compliance verification process before launch expose both themselves and their government clients to regulatory risk and potential liability.
Post-Launch Monitoring and Compliance Maintenance
Government Website Development Guidelines in India are not a one-time compliance exercise. Government websites must maintain ongoing compliance with the guidelines throughout their operational life. This means regular security updates and patches, periodic accessibility audits to verify continued WCAG compliance, content reviews to ensure accuracy and currency, performance monitoring to ensure continued compliance with load time requirements, and annual security audits and penetration tests.
Development agencies that provide post-launch maintenance services for government websites must build these ongoing compliance obligations into their maintenance service agreements and ensure that their maintenance processes include all required compliance checks and updates.
GST and Tax Considerations for Government Website Development Services
GST on Government Website Development Services
Government website development services provided by Indian agencies and technology companies attract 18 percent GST as professional and technical services. Government departments and public sector undertakings are generally registered under GST and are entitled to claim input tax credit on the GST charged by development agencies, subject to the applicable ITC eligibility rules.
Development agencies invoicing government clients for website development services must issue GST-compliant invoices that correctly identify the nature of the service, the HSN code applicable to IT services, and the applicable CGST, SGST, or IGST amounts. For development agencies registered in a state different from the government client’s location, the supply will be an inter-state supply attracting IGST rather than CGST and SGST.
TDS Deduction by Government Clients
Government departments and public sector undertakings that are registered as TDS deductors must deduct tax at source from payments made to development agencies for services under Section 194J of the Income Tax Act for professional and technical services. The TDS rate for professional services is 10 percent of the payment amount. Development agencies must account for TDS deductions from government clients in their income tax compliance and must reconcile TDS certificates received from government clients with their Form 26AS to ensure accurate income tax filing.

Common Compliance Failures in Government Website Development and How to Avoid Them
Accessibility Non-Compliance at Launch
The most common and most serious compliance failure in government website development is delivering a website at launch that does not meet the WCAG 2.1 Level AA accessibility standard. This failure typically occurs when accessibility is treated as a post-development add-on rather than being built into the design and development process from the beginning. Retrofitting accessibility onto a website that was not designed with accessibility in mind is significantly more expensive and time-consuming than building it in from the start.
Development agencies must integrate accessibility requirements into every stage of the development process including the design phase where color contrast, font sizing, and layout decisions must account for accessibility, the development phase where correct semantic HTML, ARIA labels, and keyboard navigation must be implemented, and the testing phase where both automated and manual accessibility testing must be conducted before delivery.
Security Vulnerabilities in Delivered Websites
Delivering a government website with known security vulnerabilities is a serious compliance failure that can have legal and financial consequences for the development agency under the terms of the government development contract. Security vulnerabilities discovered after launch can expose citizen data to breaches, disrupt government services, and result in significant reputational and financial damage to both the government client and the developing agency.
Security must be treated as a first-class development requirement with the same priority as functionality and performance, not as an afterthought to be addressed after the core development is complete. Conducting security code reviews, security testing, and penetration testing before delivery is non-negotiable for government website development.
Hosting on Non-Approved Infrastructure
Deploying government websites on commercial cloud platforms or shared hosting infrastructure rather than on the approved MeghRaj government cloud is a fundamental compliance failure that violates the data sovereignty and security requirements of Government Website Development Guidelines in India. Agencies that deploy on non-approved infrastructure risk having the project rejected and face potential legal liability under their government contracts.

Building a Compliant Government Website Development Practice
Investing in Accessibility Expertise
Agencies serious about winning and retaining government website development work must invest in genuine accessibility expertise within their team. This includes training developers in WCAG 2.1 compliance, hiring or contracting accessibility specialists who can conduct manual accessibility audits, and establishing accessibility review processes that are integrated into the standard development workflow.
Establishing a Security-First Development Culture
Building a security-first development culture means treating security as a core competency rather than a specialist add-on. This requires training all developers in secure coding practices, implementing code review processes that include security checks, maintaining awareness of emerging security threats and vulnerabilities in the technology stack used for government projects, and conducting regular security training and drills.
Maintaining Current Knowledge of GIGW Updates
Government Website Development Guidelines in India are updated periodically by NIC and MeIT to reflect evolving technology standards, security threats, and accessibility requirements. Agencies working in the government digital space must maintain current knowledge of the latest GIGW guidelines and update their development practices accordingly. Subscribing to NIC and MeIT communications, participating in government technology forums, and maintaining relationships with NIC officials are all important practices for staying current with Government Website Development Guidelines in India.
